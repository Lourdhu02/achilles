# 01 — Problem selection

## What a good AI-company problem looks like
- **Painful and frequent:** people do it daily and hate it, or it costs real money (labor hours, errors, compliance risk).
- **Budget exists:** someone already pays for it today (staff, BPO vendors, software). You are replacing spend, not creating a new line item.
- **AI makes it 10× better now,** not 10% better, and **it gets better as models improve.** If a better base model makes your product obsolete rather than stronger, you are a feature waiting to be absorbed.
- **You have unfair access:** you know the workflow, the buyers, the data, the language. Candidates from your history: meter and utility OCR and document automation (government and enterprise), privacy-first finance document processing (on-prem, regulated), Indic-language interfaces for MSMEs.
- **The workflow is deep:** integration into systems of record, approvals and exceptions. Depth is defensible; a chat box is not.

## Anti-patterns
Building a platform before one customer; a "wrapper" with no workflow or data advantage; head-on competition with a frontier lab's roadmap (general assistants, generic coding agents); markets where buyers can't pay (consumer apps in price-sensitive segments without a distribution edge); "AI for X" where X's users don't feel the pain.

## Validate cheaply (The Mom Test)
Ask about **past behavior**, never hypotheticals: "Walk me through the last time you did this. What did it cost? What did you try? Who signs off on buying tools?" Signals that count: time, money, or reputation committed (pilot budgets, LOIs, intros to the buyer). Compliments don't count.
**Concierge MVP:** do the job semi-manually for 2–3 customers (your SpaceDrift model), then automate what repeats. It is the fastest way to learn the real workflow and to get paid while learning.

## Worksheet (fill one per idea, keep the best 3)
Problem · who has it (the role, not the company) · how often · current solution and its cost · why now (which model capability unlocked it) · why you · the smallest paid pilot · what you'd need to believe for a ₹10 Cr or $1M ARR business · the kill criteria.
