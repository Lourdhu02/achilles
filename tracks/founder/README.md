# Track — AI founder

Most AI startups fail by building something nobody pays for, not because their model is weak. Your edge is technical depth *plus* real experience selling services (SpaceDrift) and shipping document AI to a government customer (Sujanix). This track turns that into a method.

## Sequence
1. **Earn the insight:** go deep in one domain where you see pain up close (document automation, regulated finance, Indic-language workflows are your current candidates). → [01](01-problem-selection.md)
2. **Validate before building:** 15–20 problem interviews, then pre-sales or paid pilots.
3. **Build eval-first:** frontier APIs first, your own models only when the evals or economics demand it. → [02](02-ai-product-engineering.md)
4. **Make the unit economics work** at small scale. → [03](03-unit-economics.md)
5. **Build a moat** that compounds with usage. → [04](04-moats-and-flywheels.md)
6. **Get the first 10 paying customers** yourself. → [05](05-go-to-market.md)
7. **Decide how to fund it:** bootstrap, services-to-product, or raise. → [06](06-fundraising-and-structure.md)
8. **Team and operating cadence.** → [07](07-team-and-operating.md)

## Rules
- **Keep your job until you have evidence:** signed pilots or paying customers, plus 12+ months of personal runway. **Read your employment contract's IP and moonlighting clauses before building anything on the side.**
- Work on the problem, not on "an AI startup". "We use GPT-X" is not a company.
- Every week: talk to at least one customer, ship one thing, measure one number.

## Decision gate (put it in [ROADMAP.md](../../ROADMAP.md))
At month 6 and month 9, compare the evidence: *customer pull* (paid pilots, LOIs, a waitlist that converts) vs *interview traction* (onsites, offers). Double down where the evidence is stronger; it is fine to do a frontier-lab stint first and found later with better insight and network.
