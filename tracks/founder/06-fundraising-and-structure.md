# 06 — Structure and fundraising

**Not legal or tax advice. Consult a chartered accountant and a startup lawyer before acting; rules change.**

## Structure
SpaceDrift was a **sole proprietorship**: simple, but it can't issue equity, grant ESOPs or take venture investment. To raise, incorporate a **Private Limited company** (common for Indian startups). Look into **DPIIT Startup India recognition** for benefits. If raising from US investors, you'll hear about a "Delaware flip" (a US parent company). Several large Indian startups have moved their domicile *back* to India in recent years, so evaluate current tax and exit implications with professionals before flipping.

## Funding paths
- **Bootstrap / services-to-product:** fund the product from services revenue (your SpaceDrift experience). Real risk: services swallow the product. Mitigate with a hard time budget and a product milestone.
- **Angels and pre-seed:** team, insight, early evidence. Instruments vary (SAFE-style notes in the US; Indian equivalents and priced rounds with CCPS in India; ask your lawyer).
- **Accelerators:** Y Combinator and India-focused programs. They are valuable for network and discipline.
- **Seed and Series A:** seed wants early traction; A wants a repeatable go-to-market motion.

## Founder essentials
Founder vesting (typically 4 years with a 1-year cliff), even for solo founders who later add co-founders; a clean cap table; an ESOP pool (~10%); written co-founder agreements; IP assigned to the company (and **nothing owned by your employer**).

## The pitch in one page
Problem (with numbers) → why now (the model capability) → product (demo) → evidence (pilots, retention) → market (bottom-up) → why you (domain plus core-AI depth) → the ask and the milestones it buys.
