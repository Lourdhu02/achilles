# Questions to Ask THEM

Every interview ends with "Do you have any questions for me?" **The wrong answer is "no".** The right one shows depth and gathers intel.

Have 3-5 questions ready per round type.

---

## Why this matters more than you think

The questions you ask are part of your evaluation:
- Generic questions ("what's the culture like?") = generic candidate
- Sharp questions = "this person thinks deeply about what they're joining"
- Tactical questions (about role, team) = "this person is serious"

It's also your last impression. End strong.

---

## Question banks by interviewer role

### For RECRUITER (1st screen)

Logistics, process, expectations:
1. "What's the timeline from here? When would I expect to hear about next steps?"
2. "Which teams have open positions for this profile?"
3. "What's the team match process like?"
4. "Are there typical patterns of candidates who succeed in these interviews?"
5. "What does the comp band look like for this level?"
6. "Anything I should specifically prep for that's unique to your process?"
7. "How is the team distributed (India/US/remote)?"
8. "What's the biggest reason people leave [Company]?" (the honest version of attrition)

Don't ask all 8. Pick 3-4 relevant ones.

### For HIRING MANAGER (closer to offer)

Vision, team, your role:
1. "What does success look like for someone in this role at 6 months? 12 months?"
2. "What's the most pressing problem the team is solving right now?"
3. "How does your team intersect with [other team you researched]?"
4. "What's a project you wish you had more time to spend on?"
5. "How do you make decisions about technical direction vs product asks?"
6. "What's the biggest growth opportunity for someone joining the team?"
7. "What would surprise me about working here?"
8. "How do you measure individual impact at this level?"

### For PEER ENGINEER (technical or onsite)

Day-to-day, technical depth, team dynamics:
1. "What's a technical decision the team made recently that you'd debate?"
2. "What's the gnarliest production incident you've had?"
3. "How do you handle the gap between research and production engineering?"
4. "What tools / workflows are unique to your team?"
5. "What's the code review culture like?"
6. "How do you spend your typical week between coding, design, and meetings?"
7. "What's one thing about this team you'd change if you could?"
8. "Walk me through the last bug that took longer than expected to fix."

### For SENIOR / SKIP-LEVEL (org, strategy)

Big picture:
1. "Where do you see this team / product in 2-3 years?"
2. "What's the biggest competitive bet your group is making?"
3. "What's a successful team member trait you find hardest to interview for?"
4. "How does your team prioritize technical investments vs feature work?"
5. "What's a controversial opinion you hold about the AI/ML space right now?"
6. "How does your team think about [recent industry shift, e.g., reasoning models, AGI]?"

### For BAR RAISER (Amazon specifically)

Standards, ownership, growth:
1. "How does Amazon think about maintaining the engineering bar as the company scales?"
2. "What's the most challenging part of being a Bar Raiser?"
3. "What's a Leadership Principle that's particularly important on this team right now?"
4. "How does Amazon decide what NOT to do?"

### For ANTHROPIC (values / mission-related)

Reasoning, alignment, mission:
1. "How do you balance shipping velocity with safety considerations day-to-day?"
2. "What's something Anthropic does that competitors won't / can't?"
3. "How does the company decide what models / capabilities are 'safe to ship'?"
4. "What's a paper from your team you wish more people read?"
5. "How do internal disagreements about alignment vs capability play out?"
6. "What does 'success' for Anthropic look like in 10 years?"

## Questions specific to your situation (use selectively)

### If you're worried about leveling
"What's the difference between L4 and L5 in terms of expected scope and impact?"

### If you're worried about visa / relocation
"What's the typical path for India hires to transfer to a US office?"

### If you're worried about team fit
"Could I have a brief conversation with someone on the team I'd potentially work with?"

### If you want to know about growth
"What does the promotion path look like for someone joining at this level?"

### If you want to test cultural fit
"How does the team handle disagreement about priorities?"

---

## Questions to AVOID

### Bad: questions you could Google
"What does [Company] do?" → demonstrates lack of research

### Bad: questions about benefits / WFH / leave too early
Save these for post-offer or recruiter calls. Asking technical interviewers about benefits signals priorities.

### Bad: leading or political questions
"Why is your tech stack so behind the times?" → adversarial
"What are your thoughts on [controversial topic]?" → unprofessional

### Bad: vague generic questions
"What's the culture like?" → vague answers, no signal

### Bad: questions that look desperate
"How likely am I to get this offer?" → don't go there
"When can you decide?" → recruiter question, not interviewer

---

## How to deliver questions

### Tone
- Curious, not interrogating
- Specific to what they said, when possible
- Real engagement, not performance

### Format
- 3-4 questions max per round
- Often: 2 prepared + 1-2 spontaneous based on conversation

### After they answer
- Engage with the answer ("That's interesting — does X also apply?")
- Don't just nod and move to next question
- Show you're actually listening

---

## Spontaneous questions (the best ones)

During the interview, if the interviewer mentions something interesting, ask about it later:

- They mention they switched teams 6 months ago → "What drove the team change?"
- They reference a recent product launch → "What was the biggest engineering challenge for that launch?"
- They mention scaling work → "How is the team thinking about cost as you scale?"

These show you're listening and curious — much stronger than canned questions.

---

## Per-interview prep ritual

Before EACH interview (30 min):
1. Re-research the team / interviewer (LinkedIn, blog posts)
2. Note 1-2 specifics about them you can reference
3. Pick 3-4 questions from the bank above relevant to this round
4. Have 1-2 spontaneous-feeling questions in pocket

Total: 30 min prep + you'll never have the "uh, I don't have any questions" moment.

---

## What good answers from them tell you

Watch for:
- Energy when answering: do they light up? (good team to join) or seem tired/disengaged?
- Specificity: vague answers = they don't really know the team
- Honesty about challenges: "everything's great" is a red flag

You're evaluating them too. Don't forget.
