# Recruiter Psychology

Understanding how technical recruiters at top companies actually think changes how you interact with them. This file gives you the model.

---

## What recruiters actually optimize for

Recruiters at FAANG-tier companies are evaluated on:

1. **Quality of candidates sourced** — candidates who pass interviews
2. **Time-to-fill** — how fast they close roles
3. **Acceptance rate** — % of offers that get signed
4. **Diversity / pipeline metrics** — varies by company
5. **Recruiter-to-hire ratio** — efficiency

What this means for you:
- They want you to **succeed** — your success is their KPI
- They want you to **move fast** — drawn-out timelines hurt them
- They want you to **accept the offer** — they'll work harder for candidates who seem likely to sign

If you treat the recruiter as an enemy or obstacle, you lose. They are your partner in this process — if you treat them well.

---

## What recruiters know that you don't

- **Hiring bar fluctuations:** "The bar for L4 ML in Bangalore is higher this quarter because of attrition" — they know this. You don't.
- **Hiring manager preferences:** which managers favor backgrounds like yours
- **Comp band edges:** the actual upper limit of what you can negotiate
- **Internal politics:** which teams are growing, which are frozen
- **Process timing:** when committees meet, when offers usually issue
- **Other candidates:** how strong your competition is

**Implication:** be candid with them. Ask questions. They often share more than you'd expect, if you build rapport.

## How to talk to recruiters

### Things to do
- **Be responsive** — reply within 24 hours. Slow replies signal disinterest.
- **Be specific about timeline** — "I'm targeting offers by July 2027" is better than vague
- **Be specific about comp expectations** — "Based on Levels.fyi data, I'd expect 50-65 LPA total for L4 in Bangalore" shows you've researched
- **Be honest about competing processes** — they want to know your urgency. "I have an onsite at Microsoft next week" can actually accelerate Google's process.
- **Ask questions about the role** — shows interest, gathers intel
- **Thank them after each interaction** — even via email

### Things to never do
- **Lie about other offers** — they will verify, and if caught, you're dead
- **Lie about comp** — they ask current comp at most companies (legally allowed in India). Lying = instant disqualification.
- **Ghost them** — even if you're not interested, send a polite "not pursuing right now" message. Recruiters remember.
- **Negotiate before you have an offer** — you have zero leverage until offer letter
- **Talk only about money** — signals you'd leave for any higher offer
- **Be needy or desperate** — counterproductive. Confidence (not arrogance) sells.

## The recruiter screen — what's actually being assessed

The "30-min recruiter screen" is more important than candidates realize. They are evaluating:

1. **Can you communicate?** (clear, structured speech)
2. **Are you a flight risk?** (might not accept, might leave after 6 months)
3. **Is your comp expectation in range?** (most candidates die here)
4. **Are you interested in THIS company?** (or are you carpet-bombing?)
5. **Are there any red flags?** (negative comments about past employers, vague timeline, etc.)

### Key questions they'll ask and how to answer:

**"Tell me about yourself."**
- Strong answer (60 seconds): role, key projects, key skills, why you're applying now
- "I'm Lourdu, currently a Machine Learning Engineer at Sujanix where I architect production OCR pipelines for government utility automation. Before that, I founded SpaceDrift, an AI consultancy where I delivered 40+ production ML pipelines for international clients across NLP, CV, and GenAI. My strongest current focus is GenAI — I've built ECHOME, an agentic system with novel memory architecture, and FinSentinelAI, a privacy-first enterprise RAG platform. I'm exploring [Company] because [specific reason about their work]."

**"Why are you looking to leave Sujanix?"**
- Answer with growth, not complaint. "I've learned a lot here, but [Company]'s scale of impact in [specific area] would let me work on problems at a level I can't access here."
- NEVER: "My manager is bad", "Comp is low", "Politics are tough"

**"What are your comp expectations?"**
- Have a range researched (use Levels.fyi, glassdoor, blind, your network)
- "Based on my research for [level] in [location], I'd be looking at ₹50-65 LPA total comp, with flexibility based on the specific role and team."
- If they say "out of range", ask what their range is
- If they say "what's your current comp?" — answer truthfully, but immediately reframe to your target: "Currently at ₹X, but my target for the next role is the range I mentioned."

**"When could you start?"**
- "I have a 2-month notice period at Sujanix. I'd target a start date of [N+10 weeks] from offer signing."

**"Are you interviewing elsewhere?"**
- Be honest. "Yes, I'm in early-to-mid stage with [2-3 other companies]. [Company] is among my top targets because [reason]."

**"What questions do you have for me?"**
- ALWAYS have 3+ questions. Examples:
  - "What does the interview timeline look like?"
  - "Which teams have open roles for this profile?"
  - "What's the team culture like for ML engineers — more research-flavored or more product-flavored?"
  - "What does success look like in the first 6-12 months?"

## Red flags YOU should look for in the recruiter

Not every company / process is worth your time. Watch for:

- **Vague role description** — "you might work on X or Y" → unclear team need
- **Pressure to interview immediately** without time to prep
- **Comp band refusal** — "We discuss comp after interviews" is normal, but "We can't share any range" is suspicious
- **Lack of clarity about hiring committee / timeline**
- **Bad reviews on Glassdoor / Blind about this recruiter's company specifically**

You're evaluating them too. Don't forget.

## How to manage multiple recruiters simultaneously

When you're applying to 10+ companies, you'll have 5+ recruiters active. Managing them is its own skill.

### Set up a spreadsheet (or use `08-application-strategy/05-tracker.md`):

| Company | Recruiter | Current Stage | Next Step | Date Due | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Google | Aanya | Phone screen scheduled | Sat 10am | 2027-02-15 | Asked about ML round prep |
| Amazon | Rohan | Onsite scheduled | Onsite Mar 5 | 2027-03-05 | Confirmed 5 rounds |
| Anthropic | Sarah | Values round | Awaiting feedback | 2027-02-20 | Be ready for follow-up |

### Communication patterns:

- **Don't ghost any recruiter.** Even if you're not interested anymore, send a brief "I've decided to focus on other opportunities right now, thanks for the conversation."
- **Use one recruiter to accelerate another.** "I have an onsite at Meta next week — could we move Google's phone screen up?" This works.
- **Don't lie about timing.** If you don't have a competing offer, don't say you do. Recruiters talk.

## When recruiters disappear

Sometimes after an interview round, the recruiter goes silent for weeks. This is rarely bad news; usually:

- Hiring committee scheduled late
- Recruiter on vacation
- Internal headcount review
- They're waiting on multiple candidates' onsites to compare

### What to do:
- Wait 1 week from expected response date
- Send a polite "checking in" email: "Hi [Name], I wanted to check in on the timeline for [Role]. Is there anything you need from me?"
- If no response after another week, send one more
- After 3 weeks of silence, assume rejection. Move on. Don't take it personally.

## Recruiter pet peeves (avoid these)

- Asking "what's the salary range" before they ask you
- Excessive follow-ups (1/week max if waiting)
- Asking to skip steps ("can I skip the OA?")
- Asking for accommodations without strong reasons
- Being late to scheduled calls
- Treating the screen as casual / unprepared
- Not researching the company before the call

---

## When to push back on recruiters

You can — politely — push back on:
- **Aggressive interview timelines** ("I'd like 2-3 weeks to prep" is reasonable)
- **Down-leveling** ("I'm targeting L4, not L3 — happy to demonstrate in interviews")
- **Below-market initial offers** ("Based on my research and competing processes, I'd need to see X")
- **Limited team match options** ("I'd really prefer working on Gemini Apps over Cloud DBs — can we explore that team?")

You can NOT push back on:
- The interview format (it's standardized)
- Bypassing the OA / phone screen (unless you have something exceptional)
- Specific interviewer changes

## Your script for managing relationships

### After EVERY recruiter interaction, send a thank-you email within 24 hours.

Template:
```
Hi [Name],

Thanks for the call this [morning/afternoon]. I really enjoyed our discussion about [specific topic].

To recap, the next steps are:
- [Bullet 1: their action]
- [Bullet 2: your action]

I'm looking forward to [next step]. Please let me know if you need anything else from me in the meantime.

Best,
Lourdu
```

This creates a paper trail, shows professionalism, and gives them a reason to remember you.
