# The Referral Game

Referrals are the most undervalued lever in your application strategy. They typically convert 5-10x better than cold applications. This file explains how to actually get them.

---

## What a referral buys you

| Stage | Cold apply conversion | Referral conversion |
|---|---|---|
| Resume → recruiter callback | ~2-5% | ~20-40% |
| Recruiter callback → phone screen | ~50% | ~70% |
| Phone → onsite | ~50% | ~55% |
| Onsite → offer | ~30-40% | ~30-40% (no advantage here — bar is the bar) |

**Net effect:** a referral roughly **2-4x** your total offer probability.

A referral does NOT lower the technical bar. It buys you:
- ATS bypass
- Faster recruiter response
- Some psychological "vouching" weight in early rounds
- Sometimes preview of who'll interview you

---

## Three types of referrals (from weakest to strongest)

### Type 1: Click-a-button referral
- Someone at the company just submits your resume into the referral system.
- They may or may not know you.
- Convertion bump: maybe 2x.
- This is what most "asks" on LinkedIn yield.

### Type 2: Vouched referral
- The referrer can speak to you genuinely: "I know Lourdu through X, I've reviewed his work on Y, I think he'd be a great fit."
- They might add comments to your application.
- Conversion bump: 5x.
- Earned by: prior collaboration, shared connection, demonstrating your work to them first.

### Type 3: Strong personal advocacy
- The referrer actively pushes for you: emails the hiring manager directly, brings you up in team meetings, even shares interviewer info.
- Conversion bump: 10x+.
- Earned by: having genuinely worked with them OR being deeply recommended by someone they trust.

**Your goal: aim for Type 2 minimum. Type 3 with at least 2-3 people total.**

## How to find referrers

### Step 1: List all your existing connections
- Anyone you've worked with who is now at a target company
- Anyone from your college / online community who's at a target company
- Anyone who has interacted with your GitHub / blog / Kaggle who's at a target company

For each person, note:
- Their current company + role
- Your last contact
- Strength of relationship (close colleague → distant acquaintance)

### Step 2: Identify gaps and target outreach

For target companies where you have no existing connection:
- Use LinkedIn: search "{Company name} ML engineer Bangalore" — review profiles
- Look for: 1-2 years tenure (they remember being new, more receptive), prior India experience, posts that signal openness (writing publicly, engaging in discussions)
- Look for mutual connections (2nd-degree LinkedIn)

### Step 3: Build relationships BEFORE you need referrals

Most people fail referrals because they reach out cold, asking. Better:

- Engage with their public work for 2-3 months first (comment thoughtfully on their LinkedIn posts, reply to their tweets, contribute to their OSS)
- Build genuine rapport
- Ask for advice/coffee, not for a referral
- Mention your projects naturally
- Then, when the time is right, ask

## Outreach templates (use, adapt)

### Template A: Cold LinkedIn message (warm)

```
Subject (or first line): Question about [their team / recent post]

Hi [Name],

I came across your work at [Company] — specifically your [recent post / project / contribution]. As someone building [your relevant project], I was struck by [specific insight you appreciated].

I'm a Machine Learning Engineer at Sujanix in Bengaluru with a focus on [GenAI/RAG/Agents]. My recent project ECHOME ([link]) tackles [problem in their domain] and I'd love your perspective on [specific question].

Would you be open to a 15-minute call sometime in the next 2 weeks? Happy to work around your schedule.

Thanks for your time,
Lourdu Raju
[LinkedIn] | [GitHub] | [Blog]
```

**Why this works:**
- Specific (not "I admire your work")
- Demonstrates you've engaged with their content
- Frames the ask as their expertise being valuable
- Short (3-4 lines max)
- Doesn't mention referral

### Template B: After a good conversation, asking for referral

```
Hi [Name],

Thanks again for the call last week — I really appreciated your perspective on [topic].

I wanted to follow up: I'm applying to [Company]'s [specific role]. Given our conversation about [shared technical interest], I think there's strong alignment. 

Would you be open to providing a referral? I'd be happy to send you a brief about my background and recent work — only takes 5 minutes of your time to submit through your referral system.

If not the right fit or timing, no worries — I understand referrals are weighted by trust and I'm grateful for the conversation regardless.

Best,
Lourdu
```

**Why this works:**
- Acknowledges your relationship
- Provides specific role (not "any role")
- Makes it low-effort for them
- Gives them an out
- Doesn't manipulate

### Template C: Asking via mutual connection

```
Hi [Mutual],

Hope you're doing well — I see you've been [recent thing].

I'm preparing applications to ML roles at [Company] and noticed you're connected to [Target Referrer]. I've been following [their] work in [area] and would love to have a conversation about their team.

Would you be comfortable making a brief intro? Happy to provide context on what I'd want to discuss with [them]. No pressure if not.

Thanks,
Lourdu
```

## How to BE referrable

The single biggest "secret" of referrals: **make it easy and low-risk for the referrer**.

A good referrer's mental calculation: "If I refer Lourdu and he bombs the interview, I look bad. If I refer him and he succeeds, I get a referral bonus AND credit."

To shift this calculation:
1. **Make your work legible.** GitHub READMEs that explain what/why/how. Blog posts that show your thinking. Kaggle profile with real notebooks.
2. **Make your resume polished.** They'll submit it. Don't make them embarrassed.
3. **Be specific about the role.** "I'm applying to senior ML engineer on the Bedrock team" > "any ML role".
4. **Show you've done the homework.** Mention specific projects/products of the company.
5. **Give them quotable lines.** "Lourdu has shipped 40+ production ML pipelines and just open-sourced a novel agent memory architecture" → quote-worthy in a referral system.

## When NOT to ask for referrals

- Within first 2 interactions with someone new (build relationship first)
- If you haven't engaged with their public work
- If you're asking 10+ people simultaneously and they'll notice
- If your resume isn't polished yet
- If you don't know what role you're applying to
- During holiday season / major life events visible on their LinkedIn

## Your specific 15-month referral plan

### Month 1-3: Build inbound profile
- LinkedIn: 2 posts/week (technical content)
- GitHub: clean up + 1 substantive commit/week
- Blog: 1 post/month
- Communities: actively contribute to 2-3 Discords / forums

### Month 4-6: Build network
- Add 3-5 people/week at target companies (with personalized notes)
- Engage with their content for 4-6 weeks before any ask
- Aim for: 20-30 meaningful new connections at target companies by Month 6

### Month 7-9: First referral conversations
- Target 1-2 informational conversations per week
- Don't ask for referrals yet — ask for advice, share your work
- By end of Month 9: 5-10 people who know you and your work at target companies

### Month 10-12: Activate referrals
- Apply with referrals to Wave 1-2 companies
- For each target role, ask the strongest connection at that company

### Month 13-15: Final pushes
- Even rejected applications: ask "anything I should improve before next time?" — this maintains the relationship

## The honest truth about your current referral situation

You graduated from a tier-3 college. Many of your friends are NOT at top AI companies. This is a real disadvantage.

**Mitigation paths:**
1. **OSS contributions** → people at top companies see your PRs → natural rapport
2. **Public work (blog, Kaggle, projects)** → people DM you, not the reverse
3. **Conference networking** (even online) → genuine connections
4. **Community building** (start a small Bangalore AI meetup, host a workshop) → people who attend will be at top companies

You will have to *build* your referral network. You can't inherit it.

By Month 12, your goal: **5+ strong connections per target League A company.** That's ~50+ connections in your target network. It sounds like a lot. It's ~1/week for 12 months. Doable.
