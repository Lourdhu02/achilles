# Visa & Relocation Playbook (India → US/UK/EU)

This file covers what's realistic for someone with your profile relocating from India by 2027.

---

## TL;DR

Direct relocation paths from India to US tech are limited and often slow. Realistic priority order:

1. **Get an India offer at a multinational, then internal transfer (2-4 years)** — slow, reliable
2. **Direct US offer with H1B sponsorship (lottery)** — fast but only 37% success rate annually
3. **Direct US offer with L1B (intracompany)** — requires 1+ year at the company first
4. **O1 visa (extraordinary ability)** — rare; needs publications, awards, or notable OSS
5. **Stay in India + remote work** — fastest, growing path

UK and EU paths are different (and easier in some ways than US).

---

## The US visa landscape (2026)

### H1B (specialty occupation visa)
- **Most common path for Indian engineers**
- Company must sponsor; you can't apply yourself
- **Lottery system:** ~85,000 visas/year (65k regular + 20k US masters)
- **Win rate:** ~25-37% per year for Indian applicants
- **You can be entered in the lottery 3 times** (annually, via different sponsors)
- Application window: March each year; visas issued October

**Reality:** Even if you get a US offer with H1B sponsorship, you might lose the lottery 2 years in a row. Build this risk into your plan.

### L1B / L1A (intracompany transfer)
- For employees transferred from a foreign branch to US branch of the same company
- **Requires:** at least 1 year of employment at the foreign branch in the last 3 years
- **L1B (specialized knowledge):** for individual contributors
- **L1A (managerial):** for managers/leads (3-year max with extensions to 7)
- No lottery. Faster than H1B.
- Caveat: company-dependent; companies have internal quotas

**This is why "get India offer at multinational first, then transfer" is the cleanest path.**

### O1 visa (extraordinary ability)
- For individuals with "extraordinary ability" — major awards, top publications, news media coverage, original contributions
- Hard to qualify for as a 2-YOE engineer
- **Could become relevant if you:** publish papers, have a popular OSS project (10k+ stars), win Kaggle competition, get press coverage
- Realistic for you by 2027? **Probably not.** But keep on the radar.

### Other US options (less common):
- **EB-1 / EB-2 / EB-3** (employment-based green cards) — long process, requires US employment first
- **F1 student visa** → OPT (3-year work permit if STEM) — requires US grad school first; not your path
- **TN visa** — Mexico/Canada only

## The UK visa landscape

### Skilled Worker Visa
- Company sponsors. No lottery.
- Requires offer letter, salary above threshold (~£38,700/year, sometimes lower for shortage occupations)
- Initially 5 years, can lead to settlement
- **Process:** 4-8 weeks typically. Much smoother than US H1B.

### Global Talent Visa
- For "exceptional talent" in digital tech (endorsed by Tech Nation; subject to change as Tech Nation has been wound down — check current state)
- Self-petitioned (no employer sponsorship required)
- Requires demonstrating exceptional contributions: code, talks, OSS, press
- Easier to qualify for than US O1
- **Lourdu-relevant:** If you build a strong OSS profile + public visibility, this becomes plausible by 2027

### Reality check:
- DeepMind, Anthropic London, Cohere (London office), Stability — UK-based AI companies are hireable
- UK visa process is faster and more predictable than US

## The EU landscape

### EU Blue Card
- Similar to UK Skilled Worker, requires offer with minimum salary (~€44,000-€56,000 depending on country)
- Available in: Germany, Netherlands, France, Spain, Portugal, others
- **Strong AI ecosystems:** Mistral (Paris), DeepMind (London — UK now), Hugging Face (Paris), various startups in Berlin/Amsterdam

### Country-specific options
- **Netherlands "Highly Skilled Migrant":** company sponsors, salary threshold, very efficient
- **Germany "Chancenkarte" (Opportunity Card):** points-based, can come without job offer
- **France "Talent Passport":** for skilled tech workers, family-friendly

### Reality check:
- EU paths are more accessible than US for visa
- Comp is lower than US (but cost of living also lower)
- English-language work environments increasingly common in tech

## What you should do RIGHT NOW for relocation optionality

### Months 1-6
- [ ] Get **Indian passport renewed** (if expiring within 2 years)
- [ ] Save **at least 3 months of US living expenses** (~$15,000) as emergency relocation buffer
- [ ] Take and clear the **TOEFL or IELTS** (some companies want this; valid 2 years)
- [ ] Open an **NRE account** (will help if you move)

### Months 6-12
- [ ] Build US-friendly profile: GitHub README in English with US date formats, LinkedIn set to English, USD-equivalent currency in profile bio
- [ ] If targeting UK: research Global Talent Visa requirements; start collecting evidence (OSS contributions, blog reach, etc.)
- [ ] If targeting Germany: consider learning basic German (B1 level optional but helpful)

### Months 10-15 (active application phase)
- [ ] **Apply to India offices of multinationals first** — easier visa path
- [ ] Apply to US/UK/EU offices in parallel — but expect longer processes
- [ ] During interviews, ask: "Does this role support relocation in 12-24 months?"
- [ ] Get clarity: H1B sponsorship in writing, or L1 path discussed

## The "India multinational → US transfer" play (your most realistic path)

The flow:
1. **Year 1-2 (2027-2029):** Join Google India / Microsoft India / Amazon India
2. **Year 3+ (2029+):** Apply for internal transfer to US office (L1B visa)
3. **Year 4+ (2030+):** While on L1B, file for green card (EB-2/EB-3)

**Pros:**
- No lottery
- Reliable
- Salary increases from India tier → US tier

**Cons:**
- Slow (5+ years to permanent US residence)
- Locked into one company for years
- Promotion velocity might be slower than direct US hire

### Companies known for India → US transfer programs
- Google (well-established)
- Microsoft (very common)
- Amazon (common; aggressive timelines)
- Meta (less common but possible)
- Nvidia (growing program)
- Adobe (common)

## Anthropic + OpenAI specific visa reality

Both are SF-based. Both can sponsor H1B. But:

**Anthropic** has explicit "London office" hiring for some Applied roles → easier UK visa.

**OpenAI** is more SF-centric. Most roles require US presence. They DO sponsor H1B but compete in the regular lottery.

**For you:** if applying to Anthropic/OpenAI, prioritize London (Anthropic) or remote roles. Direct SF offer with H1B is possible but visa risk is real.

## Common visa misconceptions

❌ **"If I'm good enough, the company will figure out the visa."**
✅ Reality: visa is a constraint companies factor in DURING interviews. Some teams won't hire visa-dependent candidates because of risk.

❌ **"H1B is automatic if I get an offer."**
✅ Reality: H1B is a lottery. Even with offer, 60%+ chance you don't get it that year.

❌ **"Remote work avoids visa issues."**
✅ Partly true. But many US-headquartered companies have "remote = remote within US only" policies. Verify in offer letter.

❌ **"I should marry a US citizen to fast-track."**
✅ Don't do that. Bad for you personally, doesn't actually fast-track legally as much as people think, and any whiff of fraud in immigration = decade-long ban.

## Mental model: design your timeline backwards

If your real goal is "US-based at top AI company by 2030":

```
2030: US-based at company X
2029: File EB-2 GC application
2028: On L1B from India transfer OR direct H1B win
2027: Sign offer at India multinational OR direct US offer
2026: Build profile that gets you offer
```

If your goal is "August 2027 offer, location flexible":

```
2027: Offer in hand
2026: Build profile, apply to India multinationals + US/UK/EU + remote
```

The August 2027 target is **achievable in India**. Relocation is a Year 2-5 problem, not a Year 1 problem.

## What to ask recruiters about visa

During recruiter screens, you can ask (politely):

- "Does this role support visa sponsorship?"
- "If I joined the India office, what's the typical timeline for internal transfer to US/UK?"
- "Are there teams that prefer or restrict visa-dependent candidates?"
- "Can the role be done remotely from India in the short term?"

These are normal questions. Recruiters won't penalize you for asking.

## Bottom line for your timeline

**Plan A (highest probability):** August 2027 offer at India multinational. Transfer to US 2-4 years later. Solid career, gradual relocation.

**Plan B (moderate probability):** August 2027 offer at remote AI lab (Cohere/HF/Anthropic). No visa needed, geographically free, but smaller pool.

**Plan C (stretch):** August 2027 direct US offer with H1B sponsorship → lottery in March 2028 → start October 2028 if you win. Visa risk is real.

**Plan D (long shot):** August 2027 offer + accumulate evidence + apply for O1 / UK Global Talent in 2028. Requires significant OSS / publication portfolio.

Pick one or two of these to actively pursue. Don't spread too thin.
