# Compensation & Negotiation Playbook

If you do everything right except this, you'll leave ₹10-30 LPA on the table for the next decade. Negotiation is a learnable skill.

---

## The structure of a tech offer

Total compensation = **Base** + **Bonus** + **Equity** + **Sign-on**

| Component | Description | Negotiable? |
|---|---|---|
| Base salary | Annual cash | Moderately |
| Performance bonus | % of base, paid annually based on review | Sometimes |
| Equity (RSUs) | Stock granted, vests over 4 years | Yes, often |
| Sign-on bonus | One-time cash | Very yes |
| Relocation | If moving | Yes |
| Annual refresh | New RSUs each year (FAANG) | Mostly fixed |

**Total comp (TC) for Year 1 = Base + Bonus + (Equity / 4) + Sign-on**

This is what you should compare across offers. NOT base. Most candidates make this mistake.

## Amazon's specific quirk: the equity cliff

Amazon RSU vesting (most companies):
- 5% in year 1
- 15% in year 2
- 40% in year 3
- 40% in year 4

This means your Year 1 total comp from Amazon is MUCH lower than the headline TC. To compensate, they offer a **large sign-on bonus** paid year 1 + year 2.

When comparing: **Amazon "Total Comp = ₹50 LPA"** might be ₹35 LPA in Year 1 due to vesting structure. Verify by asking the recruiter for the full breakdown.

## Negotiation principles

### Principle 1: Get the offer first
You have NO leverage until you have a verbal or written offer. Don't try to negotiate during interviews. Just perform well.

### Principle 2: Leverage is competing offers
The single biggest negotiation tool is "I have an offer from Company B at TC X." If you only have one offer, your negotiation power is limited.

**This is why you apply to multiple companies in parallel waves.**

### Principle 3: Negotiate every component
Don't just focus on base. Negotiate:
- Base
- Sign-on
- Equity refresh (Year 2-4)
- Vesting schedule
- Start date (sometimes valuable)
- Title / level

Recruiters often have flex budgets across components. If they can't raise base, they may raise sign-on.

### Principle 4: Stay calm and professional
Negotiation is not adversarial. It's collaborative problem-solving. "How do we close this gap?" is a much better frame than "I want more."

## The negotiation conversation (a template)

### Step 1: Verbal offer comes in
**Recruiter:** "We're excited to extend an offer. Total comp is ₹55 LPA..."

**You:** [DO NOT accept on the spot.]
"Thank you so much, I'm thrilled to receive this offer. Can you send me the written offer letter and full breakdown so I can review the details? When do you need a decision by?"

**Goal:** buy time. 1-2 weeks minimum.

### Step 2: Review the written offer
Look at:
- Base + bonus + equity breakdown
- Vesting schedule
- Sign-on bonus structure
- Start date
- Any clawback provisions
- Equity refresh policy

### Step 3: Indicate you want to negotiate

**You (1-2 days after offer):**
"Hi [Recruiter], thank you again for the offer. I'm very excited about [Company] and the [team] role. Before I can commit, I want to make sure the compensation reflects the market and my situation. Specifically:

- I have competing offers at [Company B] at ₹X LPA total and [Company C] at ₹Y LPA total.
- Based on Levels.fyi data for [level] in [city], the median is ₹Z LPA.
- Given my background — 2+ years of production ML, founder experience, strong GenAI portfolio — I was hoping we could move closer to ₹W LPA total.

I'm flexible on how we get there — whether through base, equity, or sign-on. What flexibility do you have?"

### Step 4: They counter
Recruiter takes it to internal team. Comes back with:
- Same offer ("This is our best")
- Slightly improved offer
- Significantly improved offer

### Step 5: Counter-counter (if needed)
If their improvement still isn't enough:

**You:**
"I appreciate the improvement. To make this work, I'd need [specific number]. Specifically, if we can [bump base to X / add sign-on of Y / improve year-1 equity by Z], I can commit today."

The "I can commit today" line is the closer. Recruiters love certainty.

### Step 6: Accept
Once the numbers work, accept verbally, get the updated written offer, then sign.

## What you can realistically negotiate (% improvement from initial offer)

| Component | Realistic uplift |
|---|---|
| Base | 5-15% |
| Sign-on | 0% (if not offered) to 50%+ if offered |
| Equity | 10-30% |
| Total comp | 10-25% typically |

If you're getting 0% improvement, either:
- You haven't shown leverage (no competing offers, no market data)
- You're already at the band ceiling
- You're being too aggressive

If you're getting 50%+ improvement, either:
- They REALLY want you
- The initial offer was lowballed and they know it

## How to get competing offers (without lying)

The biggest mistake: pretending you have offers you don't.

Better: actually have competing offers. This is why the application strategy in `00-START-HERE/03-target-companies.md` is structured in waves — to give you overlapping offers.

If you have offers from:
- League B/A peer companies → primary leverage
- A non-target company (still legitimate) → moderate leverage (e.g., Walmart Labs offer can pressure Microsoft)
- A counter-offer from your current employer → modest leverage but possible

---

## When to share competing offer details

**Share when:**
- The recruiter asks
- You want to push for higher comp
- The competing offer is genuinely strong (similar tier or higher)

**Don't share:**
- The exact written numbers if the recruiter can verify (some can, via Blind, networks)
- Without permission of the other company
- If it's a much weaker company (you'll look desperate)

**How to share:**
"I have a written offer from [Company] at ₹X LPA total comp. I'd be happy to share the details on a call but don't want to share over email."

---

## Multi-offer leverage strategy

When you have 2+ offers:

1. **Pick your top choice.** Tell them they're #1 and you want to make it work.
2. **Tell #2 about #1.** "I have an offer from #1 at X LPA. To go with you, I need [specific number/condition]."
3. **Tell #1 about #2's response.** Use the iteration cycle.
4. **Be honest about what you actually want.** "If you can get to X, I'm signing today" — and mean it.

---

## When to walk away

Negotiation has limits. Walk away when:
- They refuse to provide a breakdown
- They pressure you to decide in <24 hours after offer
- They get hostile or threatening
- Their final offer is significantly below market AND they won't budge
- Red flags during interview process compound (rude interviewers, vague role, etc.)

Walking away from a bad offer keeps your reputation and frees you for the right one.

## Specific tactics that work in India hiring

- **Notice period matters.** Bringing a 2-month notice period to a hiring manager closing Q2 is a negotiation lever. They might raise sign-on to buy you out.
- **Joining bonus is the most flexible component.** Often easier to bump than base in India.
- **Equity is sometimes negotiable up to "L+1" band.** If you're an L4 candidate and you're showing senior signals, they might give equity at L5 levels.
- **WFH / hybrid policy may be negotiable.** Especially post-2024. Get it in writing.
- **ESOP vs RSU clarity.** Some India companies offer ESOPs (private equity). Negotiate strike price, vesting, exit liquidity.

## What to do RIGHT NOW (Phase 1-2)

You're not negotiating yet. But:

1. Bookmark Levels.fyi. Check it monthly to track trends.
2. Build a spreadsheet of target compensation per company per level.
3. Note your current Sujanix comp (you'll need it for honest answers later).
4. Decide your "minimum acceptable" number. If an offer is below this, you walk.
5. Decide your "would absolutely accept" number. If an offer hits this, you sign.

The range between is where negotiation lives.
