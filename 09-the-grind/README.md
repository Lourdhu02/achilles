## The grind philosophy

> "Discipline equals freedom." — Jocko Willink

A daily routine beats motivation. Motivation is unreliable. Routines compound.

You will have:
- Weeks where you crush 35+ hours
- Weeks where life happens and you do 15
- Months where progress feels slow
- Months where everything clicks

The plan absorbs all of these. Don't quit on a bad week. Don't coast on a good one.

---

## The minimum non-negotiables

If you can ONLY do 3 things consistently, do these:

1. **Sunday planning ritual** (60 min, every week) — see `01-weekly-rituals.md`
2. **Hour tracking** (1 min, daily) — log what you actually did
3. **Monthly retrospective** (90 min, last Sunday of month) — see `03-month-checklist.md`

These 3 keep you on the rails. Everything else can flex.

## Time tracking

Recommended: simple text file or notion table. NOT complex apps.

```
Date    | DSA | ML  | GenAI | SysDes | Portfolio | Mocks | Total
2026-05-19 | 1.5 | 0.5 | 1.0   | 0      | 0.5       | 0     | 3.5
2026-05-20 | 2.0 | 0   | 0.5   | 0      | 0         | 0     | 2.5
...
```

Weekly aggregate, monthly aggregate.

**Why track:**
- Honesty (you'll otherwise overestimate)
- Pattern recognition (what topics you avoid)
- Pace check (are you on the 25/week target?)

## The energy model

You're not a machine. Energy management > time management for sustained work.

### High-energy times
- Use for: hardest tasks (new DSA patterns, system design, hard math)

### Medium-energy times
- Use for: implementation, mock prep, blog writing

### Low-energy times
- Use for: review, paper reading, LinkedIn engagement, easy LC re-solves

Don't waste your peak hours on email.

---

## Sustainability rules

1. **No "DSA every single day forever"** — burn-out
2. **Schedule weekly off-days** — even 1 day completely off prevents fatigue
3. **Social / family time non-negotiable** — humans need humans
4. **Sleep > extra hour of prep** — diminishing returns kick in fast
5. **Physical health > optimizing one more hour** — exercise, food, sunlight

You're playing a 15-month game. Pace accordingly.
