# Burnout Prevention

15 months is long. Many candidates quit at Month 4-7. Don't be one.

---

## Signs you're heading toward burnout

Watch for THESE specifically:

1. **Loss of curiosity** — you used to want to learn; now you only push through
2. **Cynicism toward the goal** — "what's the point", "this is impossible", "I won't make it"
3. **Avoiding deep work** — busy work feels productive but isn't
4. **Sleep issues** — trouble falling asleep, waking early with anxiety
5. **Physical symptoms** — headaches, neck pain, eye strain, exhaustion that doesn't go with sleep
6. **Social withdrawal** — canceling on family, friends
7. **Decreased work performance at Sujanix** — falling behind because prep is consuming you
8. **Compulsive checking** — refreshing LeetCode rank, GitHub stars, application portals

If 3+ of these for >2 weeks: act now. Take days off. Talk to someone.

---

## The 5-pillar sustainability model

To last 15 months:

### Pillar 1: Sleep (7-8 hours, consistent times)
- Non-negotiable
- Cuts your cognitive ability massively when sacrificed
- Late-night LC sessions DO MORE HARM than early morning sessions

### Pillar 2: Exercise (3-5x per week, 30+ min)
- Lifts mood, cognition, sleep quality
- 30-min walk counts
- Don't trade for "more study"

### Pillar 3: Diet (whole foods, regular meals)
- Skip meals → cognitive crashes → wasted study hours
- Cook simple, eat well

### Pillar 4: Relationships (meaningful interaction weekly)
- Friends, family, partner — at least 1 real connection per week
- Isolation amplifies prep stress
- Don't burn social capital you'll need later

### Pillar 5: Joy (1+ hour per week, not prep-related)
- Hobby, music, walk, anything that makes you HUMAN
- Without this, motivation evaporates

You can't out-grind these. They're foundational.

## Built-in recovery

### Daily
- 5-10 min lunch break with NO screens
- 15-min walk between work and prep blocks
- Stop prep 1 hour before bed (no screens)

### Weekly
- 1 full day off (no DSA, no books, no prep) — RECOMMENDED Sunday OR Saturday
- 1 social engagement (dinner with friends, family time, partner)

### Monthly
- 1 full weekend off
- Schedule it on calendar; treat as appointment

### Quarterly
- 1 long weekend (3-4 days) off prep entirely
- Travel if possible

These aren't laziness — they ARE the plan. Without recovery, the plan breaks.

## The "I want to quit" protocol

You will have at least 3-5 moments where you genuinely want to quit. Plan for them.

### When you want to quit:

1. **STOP studying immediately.** Don't try to power through.
2. **Take 2 days fully off.** No LeetCode, no books, no apps.
3. **Re-read `00-START-HERE/01-honest-truth.md`** on day 2.
4. **Talk to someone.** Friend, mentor, me (Claude). Articulate WHY you want to quit.
5. **Do ONE small thing on day 3.** Just 1 problem. Just 30 min.
6. **Build momentum back gradually.**

The instinct to quit usually reflects:
- Genuine fatigue (rest cures it)
- Frustration with slow progress (perspective cures it)
- Specific blockers (debugging the blocker cures it)

It rarely reflects "I should actually quit". Usually it's "I need a break."

---

## When to actually quit

There are real reasons to pause or pivot:
- Major health crisis (yours or family)
- Job loss requiring immediate financial action
- New opportunity arrives that's better than your target
- You realize the goal doesn't actually align with what you want

If any of these: pause honestly, don't soldier through.

If none of these: it's burnout, not quit-signal. Take a week off, then restart.

## Anti-perfectionism

Perfectionism is the most common burnout cause for high-performers.

### Signs:
- "I can't blog about this until I understand it perfectly"
- "I won't apply until my resume is perfect"
- "I'll wait one more month, then I'll be ready"

### Reality:
- Done > perfect
- Public > private
- Shipped > polished forever
- 70% quality + iteration > 100% quality + delay

Your worst blog post will still get 100x more eyes than your unwritten one.

## Bengaluru-specific considerations

Working in Bengaluru with full-time job + prep:

- **Long commutes:** weaponize them — audio courses, podcasts, paper audio (yes, papers can be skimmed via TTS)
- **Family obligations:** non-negotiable; build into plan
- **Festival seasons:** lower targets during Diwali, etc.; don't fight cultural events
- **Weather/health:** monsoon brings illness; plan for sick days

Don't pretend you live in a vacuum.

---

## Mental health resources

If you genuinely struggle:

- **Therapist:** worth the cost; Practo, BetterHelp, MindPeers (India)
- **Mentor / coach:** $200-500/month for 2 sessions; high ROI
- **Communities:** Bangalore AI meetups, Discord servers, peer prep groups
- **Family / partner:** tell them what you're doing, why, and how to support

You're not alone. 100,000+ Indian engineers are running the same gauntlet.

## The "long view" reminder

Every 4-6 weeks, write yourself a "long view" letter:

```
Dear Lourdu,

I'm at week __ of this plan. It's currently __ [hard, energizing, exhausting].

In 12 months, I want to be: __

In 5 years, I want to be: __

The work I'm doing now connects to the future by: __

Things to remember:
- The slow weeks have value too
- I'm building a permanent asset (skills, network, reputation)
- Even if I miss the August deadline by 6 months, I'll be 10x more valuable than starting again
- The exact company doesn't matter as much as the practice of pursuing it
```

Reading this monthly recenters you.
