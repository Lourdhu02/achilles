## Resource philosophy

**Less is more.**

Most candidates drown in resources. They watch 10 tutorials on the same topic. They buy 5 books they never read.

You will:
- Pick 3-5 books TOTAL across the 15 months
- Complete 3-5 courses (not "consume" — complete)
- Subscribe to 5-10 high-signal channels
- Ignore 90% of new "must-read" tweets

**Time spent consuming > time spent building = wrong ratio.**

Aim for 70% building (DSA, projects, mocks) + 30% consuming.

---

## The "consume → produce" loop

Every resource you consume should produce something:
- Read a book → take notes, write a summary blog post
- Take a course → implement the final project
- Read a paper → write a 5-bullet summary
- Watch a YouTube video → implement what you learned

If consumption doesn't produce output, it's not learning. It's procrastination.

## Resource priority pyramid

```
                ╱╲
               ╱  ╲     TIER 1 — Active work
              ╱    ╲    (your projects, your mocks, your problem solving)
             ╱──────╲   
            ╱        ╲  TIER 2 — Targeted reading/courses
           ╱          ╲ (filling specific gaps)
          ╱────────────╲
         ╱              ╲ TIER 3 — Habits / continuous input
        ╱                ╲(weekly newsletter, monthly podcast)
       ╱──────────────────╲
      ╱                    ╲ TIER 4 — Background noise (drown out)
      ──────────────────────
```

80% of your time should be Tier 1. Most candidates flip this — they're 80% Tier 3/4.

---

## How to evaluate any new resource

Before spending time on a new course / book / paper, ask:

1. **What specific gap does this fill?** (If you can't name it, skip)
2. **What will I produce after consuming it?** (If nothing, skip)
3. **How does it fit my current phase?** (Phase 1: skip GenAI deep stuff. Phase 2: skip more DSA basics. Etc.)
4. **Is it the best option?** (Don't reflexively buy popular)
5. **Can I dedicate the time?** (A 12-hour course you don't finish = 0 value)

If yes to all 5: invest. Otherwise: bookmark and move on.
