# Books — Curated, Prioritized

5-7 books to buy/borrow across 15 months. Don't try to read 20.

---

## Tier 1: BUY THESE (worth your time AND money)

### 1. "Designing Machine Learning Systems" by Chip Huyen (2022)
- **Why:** Best-in-class for ML systems thinking
- **When to read:** Month 5-6
- **Time:** ~25 hours
- **What you'll get:** Production ML mental models, system design depth

### 2. "Machine Learning System Design Interview" by Ali Aminian (2023)
- **Why:** Practical, interview-focused, worked examples
- **When:** Month 5-7
- **Time:** ~15 hours
- **What you'll get:** Templates for ML system design interview answers

### 3. "Hands-On Large Language Models" by Jay Alammar & Maarten Grootendorst (2024)
- **Why:** Best LLM practical book; code-heavy
- **When:** Month 4-5
- **Time:** ~30 hours (with implementation)
- **What you'll get:** Implementation-level LLM knowledge

### 4. "Cracking the Coding Interview" by Gayle McDowell
- **Why:** Classic for DSA + interview behavior. Still relevant.
- **When:** Month 1-3
- **Time:** ~30 hours (do problems, not just read)
- **What you'll get:** Foundations + interview psychology

### 5. "AI Engineering" by Chip Huyen (2025)
- **Why:** Newer, LLM/GenAI-focused successor to her earlier book
- **When:** Month 6-8
- **Time:** ~20 hours
- **What you'll get:** Latest thinking on GenAI engineering systems

---

## Tier 2: WORTH BORROWING (read selectively)

### 6. "Deep Learning" by Ian Goodfellow, Yoshua Bengio, Aaron Courville (2016)
- **Why:** The textbook. Comprehensive.
- **When:** Reference, not cover-to-cover
- **Time:** As needed for specific topics
- **What you'll get:** Mathematical foundations for DL

### 7. "Pattern Recognition and Machine Learning" by Christopher Bishop (2006)
- **Why:** Foundational classical ML
- **When:** Reference
- **Time:** As needed
- **What you'll get:** Bayesian / probabilistic ML depth

### 8. "Designing Data-Intensive Applications" by Martin Kleppmann (2017)
- **Why:** System design bible (non-ML); useful for "system" half of ML system design
- **When:** Month 6-8
- **Time:** ~30 hours
- **What you'll get:** Distributed systems thinking

---

## Tier 3: Specific Tactical Reads

### "Acing the AI Interview" / "Machine Learning Interviews" by Khang Pham (free online)
- **Why:** Lots of practice Q&A
- **When:** Month 2-10
- **Time:** As needed

### "Grokking the Coding Interview" by Educative (course/ebook)
- **Why:** Excellent pattern-based DSA explainer
- **When:** Month 1-3
- **Time:** ~40 hours

### "Generative Deep Learning" by David Foster (2nd ed., 2023)
- **Why:** Practical generative models
- **When:** Skip unless interviewing for image/multi-modal specialist

- **fast.ai's "Practical Deep Learning"** — free online course + book
- **CS229 lecture notes (Andrew Ng)** — free PDF
- **CS231N (Stanford CV)** — free
- **CS224N (Stanford NLP)** — free
- **Andrej Karpathy's nanoGPT + neural networks zero-to-hero** — best free DL teaching

---

## What NOT to read (saves you hundreds of hours)

- "AI for Dummies" / similar — too superficial
- "Learn TensorFlow in 30 days" — superficial, plus tooling shifts
- Old (pre-2020) NLP books — pre-transformer obsolete
- Most pop-AI books (e.g., predictions of AGI) — entertainment, not learning
- Resume "advice" books — read this repo's `07-portfolio/` instead

---

## Reading rhythm

For active books (Tier 1):
- 1 chapter per week
- Take notes (Obsidian / plain markdown / paper)
- Implement key concepts in code
- Write a 1-paragraph summary per chapter

For reference books (Tier 2):
- Open when you encounter a specific gap
- Read only relevant section
- Move on

This is more effective than "I'll read every book cover-to-cover."
