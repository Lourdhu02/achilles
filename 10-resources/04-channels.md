# Channels — YouTube, Podcasts, Blogs, Communities

5-10 high-signal sources. Ignore everything else.

---

## YouTube (educational + research)

### Tier 1 (subscribe + watch new uploads)
- **Andrej Karpathy** — long-form deep teaching (neural networks zero-to-hero, LLM lectures)
- **3Blue1Brown** — math intuition (linear algebra, calculus, NN, transformers)
- **Yannic Kilcher** — paper deep dives, opinionated takes
- **Jay Alammar** — illustrated explainers (visual learners)

### Tier 2 (selective)
- **AI Coffee Break** — quick paper summaries (~10 min each)
- **Two Minute Papers** — high-energy paper highlights (very short)
- **NeurIPS / ICML / ICLR** official channels (talk recordings)
- **Stanford Online** — CS229, CS231N, CS224N lectures

### Tier 3 (entertainment, not learning)
- **Lex Fridman Podcast** — long interviews, occasional ML insight
- **Big Think AI panels** — generally surface-level

**Avoid the influencer-y AI channels.** "I tried 10 LLMs you won't believe" videos waste time.

## When to UNSUBSCRIBE

After 1 month with a channel:
- Has it taught you something useful? Yes → keep
- Are you skipping past it? Yes → unsubscribe
- Does it stress you out? (FOMO, doomerism, hype) → unsubscribe

Don't accumulate noise. Curate ruthlessly.

---

## Anti-pattern: information addiction

Common failure: spending 2 hrs/day on Twitter and feeling "productive."

You're not. You're consuming, not building.

Cap consumption at 1 hr/day. Spend the rest building.
