# The Application Funnel — Numbers That Matter

Realistic numbers for a well-prepared candidate like you.

---

## The base case funnel

```
150 applications (across Waves 1-4)
  ↓ ~20-25% conversion
30-40 recruiter callbacks
  ↓ ~50% conversion
15-20 phone screens
  ↓ ~50% conversion
6-10 onsites
  ↓ ~30-40% conversion
2-4 offers
```

End state: 1 dream job, choice of multiple.

---

## Conversion rates by path

| Path | Resume → Callback | Callback → Phone | Phone → Onsite | Onsite → Offer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cold apply | 2-5% | 50% | 50% | 30-40% |
| Recruiter inbound | 70-80% | 80% | 50% | 30-40% |
| Strong referral | 30-50% | 80% | 60% | 30-40% |
| Internal transfer | N/A | N/A | High | Higher |

**The Onsite→Offer rate is roughly the same regardless of path.** The path affects how MANY onsites you get. Volume of interviews compounds.

---

## What "150 applications" actually means

You're not blasting Indeed. You're applying thoughtfully:

- 10 League A companies × 5-10 roles each = 50-100 applications
- 7 League B companies × 3-5 roles each = 20-35 applications
- 5-10 other relevant companies × 2-3 roles = 15-30 applications

Some role variants exist (Sr. ML Eng vs Applied Scientist vs Research Eng). Apply to multiple at the same company if profile matches.

## Where the funnel breaks down (debugging)

### Symptom: 200 applications, 0 callbacks
**Diagnose:**
- Resume issues (most likely): check `07-portfolio/01-resume-feedback-on-yours.md`
- ATS keyword mismatch
- Wrong roles (over- or under-leveled)
- No referrals
- Profile sketchy in some way (Glassdoor reviews of you, weird LinkedIn etc.)

**Fix:** improve resume, get 2-3 senior reviews, target referrals before pure cold apply.

### Symptom: callbacks but no phone screens
**Diagnose:**
- Comp expectation mismatch (most common; ATS flagged you out)
- Recruiter screen poor (your "tell me about yourself" weak)
- Bad fit for the role you were sourced for

**Fix:** practice recruiter screen, calibrate comp ask vs market, only accept callbacks where role matches.

### Symptom: phone screens but no onsites
**Diagnose:**
- DSA gap (most likely for ML Eng phone screen)
- Communication issues in technical talk
- Bombed a specific question

**Fix:** mock more, debrief each one ruthlessly, target the weakest pattern.

### Symptom: onsites but no offers
**Diagnose:**
- Specific round repeatedly weak (coding? system design? behavioral?)
- Hiring committee borderline (Lean Hire across rounds = no offer)
- Cultural/values mismatch (more likely Anthropic-tier)

**Fix:** ask recruiter for feedback after rejections; target the weak round.

### Symptom: offers but at lower comp / level than wanted
**Diagnose:**
- Down-leveled (you came across as L3 when applying for L4)
- Below-market negotiation

**Fix:** review behavioral stories for "scope" signals; negotiate harder using competing offers.

## "Should I apply if I'm not 100% qualified?"

Yes.

Job descriptions are aspirational. Hiring managers expect candidates to be 70% match.

Rule of thumb:
- If you meet 70% of "Required" qualifications: apply
- If you meet 50%: still apply if your strengths are uniquely strong elsewhere
- If you meet <50%: skip unless you have a strong referral

Underqualified candidates often get hired; overqualified rarely get applied to (overconfidence problem).

---

## "Should I apply to 5 roles at the same company?"

Only if you genuinely match each role.

Common pattern at FAANG:
- 1 application = your strongest match
- 2 applications = your strongest + 1 secondary match
- 3+ = signals desperation; recruiter consolidates anyway

Better: 1 well-targeted application per company, with a referral if possible.

---

## Volume gone wrong

Common mistake: applying to 30 roles in a week.
- Each application gets 5 min of effort
- Resumes are generic
- No referrals
- ATS noise

Result: 0 callbacks. Demoralized.

**Better:** 5 carefully-targeted applications per week, with referrals attempted at each.

---

## The dark art: parallel pipeline acceleration

When you get callbacks at multiple companies, USE THEM:

- "I have an onsite at Microsoft next week. Could we accelerate Google's process?"
- Most recruiters will move you up the priority queue
- This gets you to "offer comparison" stage faster
- Compresses 6-month process into 8-10 weeks

This is the #1 reason to apply in BATCHES, not sequentially.
