## Interview formats (durable patterns)

These patterns have held for several years; confirm the current loop for each company in its guide and with the recruiter.

- Frontier labs lean on practical coding, ML deep dives, system design, and a values or mission conversation, with less emphasis on puzzle-style algorithm questions.
- Big Tech usually keeps data-structures-and-algorithms rounds, plus ML design and behavioral rounds tied to published principles.
- Almost everyone probes past projects deeply: every résumé claim gets tested ([résumé guide](resume/guide.md)).

Preparation: [interview loops](../tracks/research-engineer/interview-loops.md), [question bank](../tracks/research-engineer/question-bank.md), [stories](stories.md).

## How to research a team

1. **Careers page:** the exact role text, location, level, and the team's name. Save a copy; postings disappear.
2. **Recent output:** the team's papers (author lists name the people), blog posts, open-source repos and talks from the last 12 months.
3. **People:** find 2–3 engineers on the team; read what they have published. This is also your [outreach](outreach.md) list.
4. **Tools they use:** frameworks and infrastructure visible in their code and job posts. Contribute to one of them if you can ([public presence](public-presence.md#open-source)).
5. **Conversations:** one 20-minute chat with a current or former employee is worth more than hours of reading.

Per-team note (keep in your tracker):

```text
Company · team · date researched · sources
What the team shipped or published in the last 12 months (links)
The problem they seem to be working on next
People I could learn from or contact
Which of my labs / write-ups is most relevant, and the gap to close
Interview format as reported (source, date)
Location, remote, visa sponsorship (source, date)
```
