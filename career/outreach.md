# Outreach: referrals and cold contact

A referral from someone who knows your work is the strongest way into a competitive team. This file shows how to build those relationships before you need them, and gives annotated message templates for each situation.
**Principle:** make it easy and low-risk for someone to vouch for you. That means legible public work (labs, write-ups, pull requests) and a specific, small ask.

**Contents:** [Build the network](#build-the-network-before-you-need-it) · [Templates](#templates-annotated) · [Follow-up rules](#follow-up-rules) · [Tracking](#tracking) · [Don'ts](#donts)

## Build the network before you need it

- **Engage with people's public work for weeks** before any ask: a thoughtful comment, a reproduction of their result, a pull request to their repo, a question that shows you read the paper.
- **Contribute in communities** where core-AI engineers spend time: GPU MODE, EleutherAI, Hugging Face forums and Discord, open-source repos you use in the labs, and local meetups in Bengaluru.
- **Aim for about one genuine new relationship per week.** After a year that is around 50 people who have seen your work.
- **Give before asking:** share a benchmark result someone would find useful, fix a doc bug in their project, answer questions in the communities above.

> [!TIP]
> The best first message contains a result the recipient did not have before: "I reproduced your figure 3 on a consumer GPU and the effect shrinks at small batch sizes; here's the notebook." It gives them a reason to reply and shows the work you would do on their team.

## Templates (annotated)

Keep every message under 120 words. The bracketed numbers explain why each line exists.

### 1. First contact (no ask)

```text
Hi <name>, I read your <post / paper / PR> on <topic>.                 [1]
I reproduced <specific result> on <hardware> and found <one concrete    [2]
observation> (<link>).
Curious whether you saw <specific question>?                           [3]
No ask beyond that; thanks for publishing it.                          [4]
```
1. Shows you engaged with *their* work, not their employer's brand.
2. Evidence of your ability, delivered as something useful to them.
3. A question that is easy and interesting to answer invites a reply.
4. Removes the pressure. Relationships built this way lead to referrals later.

### 2. Asking for a referral after a conversation

```text
Thanks again for the chat about <topic>.                                [1]
I'm applying to <exact role, link> on <team>.                          [2]
Given <the relevant work you know I've done>, would you be comfortable  [3]
referring me?
Here's a 3-line summary you can paste: <summary>.                       [4]
Totally fine if not.                                                    [5]
```
1. Anchors the request in a real prior interaction.
2. An exact role and link: the referrer doesn't have to search.
3. Reminds them what they can vouch for, and asks about comfort, not obligation.
4. Makes referring a two-minute task. Write the summary in third person, with links.
5. A graceful exit keeps the relationship intact either way.

### 3. Introduction via a mutual contact

```text
Hi <mutual>, would you be open to introducing me to <target>?          [1]
I'm working on <specific overlap> and would value 15 minutes of their   [2]
perspective on <question>.
Happy to send a short blurb you can forward.                            [3]
```
1. Asks the mutual contact first, so they can decline privately.
2. A specific reason and a small time request.
3. A forwardable blurb (3 lines, with a link) saves the mutual contact the work.

### 4. Cold message to a hiring manager

```text
<One line on a specific thing their team shipped or published.>          [1]
<One line of evidence you can do that work, with a link to your artifact.> [2]
Is <role> still open, and is this the kind of background you're          [3]
looking for?
```
1. Proves relevance in the first line; hiring managers skim.
2. The artifact carries the message; adjectives don't.
3. A yes/no question that is easy to answer, even with "no, but talk to X".

### 5. Replying to a recruiter who contacted you

```text
Thanks for reaching out. I'm interested in <team / area> and would be    [1]
glad to talk. For context, my recent work is <one line + link>.         [2]
I'm available <two or three time slots with time zone>.                  [3]
```
1. Signals interest and steers toward the team you want.
2. Gives the recruiter something to pass to the hiring manager.
3. Removes a round of scheduling messages. Then use the [recruiter-call script](applications.md#recruiter-call-script).

## Follow-up rules

- **One follow-up**, 7–10 days later, adding something new (a result, a relevant write-up). Then stop.
- **Thank people who helped** and tell them the outcome, whatever it was. People who referred you want to know.
- **Keep your promises:** if you said you would send a blurb or a notebook, send it the same day.

## Don'ts

"Please refer me for any role"; a résumé attached to a first message; the same message to ten people at one company; more than one follow-up; asking for a referral from someone who has never seen your work; claiming a connection that does not exist.
