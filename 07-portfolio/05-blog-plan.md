# Blog Content Plan — 12 Months, 12 Posts Minimum

A blog is your most defensible long-term differentiator. Recruiters quote your blog posts back to you in interviews. Hiring managers Google you and find your writing.

---

## Why blog (not just LinkedIn / Twitter)

- **Permanence:** posts compound; SEO; lives forever
- **Depth:** longer-form than social
- **Owned platform:** algorithm can't bury you
- **Credibility signal:** writing IS thinking; posts demonstrate both
- **Career insurance:** if you lose your job tomorrow, your blog is still attracting recruiters

---

## Platform choice

### Recommended: own domain + simple static site
- `lourduraju.com` or `lourdhuraju.dev`
- Tools: Astro, Hugo, Next.js, Eleventy
- Hosted on Vercel / Netlify / Cloudflare Pages (free)
- 1-time setup: ~4-6 hours

### Acceptable: Substack
- Easier setup
- Built-in email list (valuable)
- Less customization
- Subscribers harder to migrate elsewhere

### Acceptable: Medium / dev.to / Hashnode
- Cross-post for additional reach
- Don't be ONLY here (you don't own the audience)

### Best practice: own domain + cross-post to Medium/dev.to with canonical URL

## Post structure (template)

```markdown
# Title (clear, specific, searchable)

> Subtitle that previews the takeaway

[Hero image or diagram]

## The hook (1-2 paragraphs)
Why should the reader care? What problem does this solve / interest does this engage?

## Context (2-4 paragraphs)
Background, related work, why this is non-trivial.

## The meat (5-15 paragraphs)
Detailed technical content. Include:
- Code snippets (highlight tradeoffs)
- Diagrams
- Numbers / benchmarks
- "What I tried and why it failed" sections

## Takeaways (1-2 paragraphs)
What should the reader walk away with?

## What's next (1 paragraph)
Future work / related posts / call to action

---

Liked this? Subscribe / Follow me on LinkedIn / X / etc.
```

---

## Length

- 1500-2500 words for technical deep-dives
- 800-1200 words for quick takes
- Don't pad. Cut ruthlessly.

---

## Distribution per post

For each post, 1-hour distribution effort:

1. **Cross-post to dev.to + Hashnode + Medium** (with canonical URL)
2. **LinkedIn post** — 200 words summary + link
3. **X / Twitter thread** — 5-10 tweet thread
4. **Hacker News submission** — if technical and interesting
5. **/r/MachineLearning or /r/LocalLLaMA** — when relevant
6. **Email to 2-3 people who'd care** — direct engagement

The blog post itself is 80% of effort. Distribution is the other 20% but produces 80% of the reach.

---

## SEO basics

- Title: clear, specific, includes keywords
- URL slug: short, hyphenated
- Meta description: 150 chars
- Headers: H1 (title) > H2 (sections) > H3 (subsections)
- Internal links: link to your other posts
- External links: cite sources, give credit

## Common pitfalls

- **Imposter syndrome:** "everyone knows this already"
  → NO. Beginners don't. Recruiters don't. Write for them.
- **Trying to be perfect:** spending 30 hours per post
  → 8-12 hours is the sweet spot. Ship, iterate.
- **Writing without distribution:** post in the void
  → Always distribute. Otherwise no compounding.
- **Plagiarism risk:** rephrasing tutorials
  → Add YOUR insights, YOUR experience, YOUR code

## Tracking

Per post log:
- Date
- Title
- Word count
- Views (week 1, month 1, all-time)
- Engagement (comments, shares)
- Inbound from this post (recruiters, opportunities)

Identify what works → write more of that.

---

## When you can't think of what to write

- A recent bug you debugged
- A paper you read this week + your take
- A tool comparison (e.g., vLLM vs TGI)
- "I tried X and it didn't work — here's why"
- A tutorial for a thing you just learned (teach to lock it in)

Every week you do something blog-worthy. Capture it.
