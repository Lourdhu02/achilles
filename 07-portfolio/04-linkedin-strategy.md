# LinkedIn Strategy

LinkedIn is the #1 inbound channel for tech recruiters. Optimize aggressively.

---

## Profile setup (do this in Month 1, 4-6 hours)

### Photo
- Professional headshot (same as GitHub)
- Clear, well-lit, smiling

### Banner
- Subtle, on-brand
- Could include: tagline, code snippet, abstract tech imagery
- Don't put text overload

### Headline (1 line, critical — appears in recruiter search)
Format: `Role at Company | Specialty 1 · Specialty 2 · Specialty 3`

Examples for you:
- "ML Engineer at Sujanix | GenAI · Agentic AI · Computer Vision | Kaggle Expert"
- "Machine Learning Engineer | Building production AI systems in GenAI + CV"

Test both, see which gets more recruiter views.

### About section (3-5 paragraphs)
- Paragraph 1: WHO you are (role, focus, location)
- Paragraph 2: WHAT you've shipped (projects + impact)
- Paragraph 3: WHAT you're interested in (mention target areas)
- Paragraph 4: HOW to reach you
- Optional Paragraph 5: Brief mission statement / values

### Experience section
- Mirror resume but with more detail per role (LinkedIn allows more space)
- Each role: 4-6 bullet points
- Add media: blog posts, GitHub links, slides
- For SpaceDrift: explicitly list 2-3 example projects

### Featured section (the "hero shelf")
Pin 4 items:
1. Your best blog post
2. ECHOME or FinSentinelAI repo
3. A LinkedIn post you wrote that got engagement
4. Kaggle profile or notable competition

This is the first thing recruiters see when they land on your profile.

### Skills section
- Top 5: GenAI, LLMs, PyTorch, RAG, Computer Vision (or similar)
- Get endorsements (your colleagues, network)
- Don't list everything — focus

### Recommendations
- Aim for 3-5 by end of Phase 2
- Ask former colleagues, clients (SpaceDrift), mentors
- Give first to receive

## Connection strategy

### Quality over quantity
- 500+ connections is the LinkedIn "open" threshold
- 1000+ is "established"
- 2000+ shows real network

### Who to connect with
- Current + former colleagues
- People at target companies (slowly — see `01-hiring-process/05-the-referral-game.md`)
- People who engage with your content
- Recruiters who reach out (always accept; they're a future resource)
- Conference speakers / authors you've engaged with

### How to send connections
**Always include a custom note** (10-30 words). Examples:
- "Hi [Name], I really enjoyed your post on [X]. Connecting to follow more of your work."
- "Hi [Name], we're both in the GenAI engineering space in Bangalore. Connecting to stay in touch."

NEVER blank invites. Acceptance rates drop drastically.

### Cadence
- 5-10 thoughtful connections/week
- Don't blast

## Content strategy

This is where most engineers underperform. Content compounds.

### Why post

- Build reputation as a thoughtful engineer
- Get recruiter inbound
- Become discoverable to your target community
- Build "social proof" before you apply

### What to post

#### Tier 1: Technical insights from your work
- "Lessons from building production RAG"
- "Why we chose Qdrant over Chroma at scale"
- "What I learned implementing FocalCTCLoss"

These are HIGH-value posts. 1-2x per month.

#### Tier 2: Paper / news commentary
- "DeepSeek's MLA is more interesting than people realize — here's why"
- "Mistral Large 3 release — first thoughts"

Quick takes, 2-4 per month.

#### Tier 3: Engineering humor / culture
- "Things only ML engineers will understand..."
- "When your loss plateaus on day 3..."

Lighter, but still on-brand. Sparingly.

#### Tier 4: Achievements
- "Just shipped X"
- "Just open-sourced Y"

OK monthly. Don't over-do.

### Anti-patterns
- Generic "Excited to announce!" without substance
- Repost low-quality content
- Engagement bait ("Like if you agree!")
- Personal venting / drama
- Pure self-promotion every post

### Post format

Recommended structure for technical posts:

```
[Hook — 1-2 lines that make scrollers stop]

[Problem — 2-3 lines]

[Solution / insight — 5-8 lines with specific details]

[Result / takeaway — 2-3 lines]

[Optional: code snippet or image]

#tag #tag #tag (3-5 relevant)
```

### Length
- Mobile-first: ideal post is 5-10 short paragraphs
- Use line breaks generously (LinkedIn dense paragraphs read poorly)
- 1500 chars max for "see more" cut-off

### Cadence
- 2-3 posts/week (optimal LinkedIn algorithm engagement)
- Comment on 5-10 posts/day (algorithm boost + visibility)
- 30 min/day on LinkedIn = 3-5 hr/week investment

---

## Engagement strategy

### Comments matter as much as posts
- Long thoughtful comments on others' posts get YOU visibility
- Become known in your target community by being the smart commenter
- Comment on posts from: target companies' engineers, AI researchers, conference speakers

### Engagement budget
- 30 min/day: 10-15 thoughtful comments + 1 post (every other day)
- Builds your visible network over time

---

## What you should NEVER do on LinkedIn

- Argue with strangers (always reflects badly on you)
- Vent about your job (recruiters see everything)
- Engage with political / divisive content under your professional account
- Post about salary specifics
- Bash former employers or colleagues
- "Open to work" badge IF you're employed and want to keep it quiet
- Engage with engagement-baiting "agree?" posts

## Activity log (track yourself)

Update weekly:

- Comments this week: ___

- Recruiters who reached out: ___
- Inbound interview opportunities: ___

By Month 12, you should be receiving 5-10 recruiter messages per week.

---

## LinkedIn for application strategy

When applying to a role:
1. Find 1-2 employees at the target company
2. Send connection (with personalized note about the role / their work)
3. Wait for acceptance, comment on their content for 2 weeks
4. Then ask for referral (`01-hiring-process/05-the-referral-game.md`)

This pipeline is your best application-to-interview converter.
